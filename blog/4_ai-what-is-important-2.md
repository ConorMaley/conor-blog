### More thoughts about what the future holds for software engineers.

---

> the ability to translate a domain model into working code, is now cheap

I read [this article off HN](https://www.brethorsting.com/blog/2026/05/domain-expertise-has-always-been-the-real-moat/) and this story popped into my head:

A good friend of mine, an avid golfer and attorney by trade who professionally is as far away from any sort of tech, spent a few hundred bucks (let's say $200) on Claude and built a website to help golfers book golf trips and it looks like great. He has a whole business plan sketched out for digital, online, golf trip planning app and _never_ consulted a human engineer.

Jean Sammet (GOAT) envisioned the idea of using [natural language for programming](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/365230.365274) 4 years before the Unix timer started (March 1st, 1966!) and I'd love to know what she'd think about the state of programming today. Her ideology is thriving today English as a programming language enables all english speakers to be engineers if they so choose, super cool! 

The value of the _programmer_ has decreased, yet the value of the domain expert and system designer remains the same (if not increases, due to the speed of which implementation can be performed!). Domain knowledge, shifting _left_ (not in the testing sense, more on that in another article), is going to be what's valuable for engineers to flourish in the near future. And someone who both understands _what_ technology can do a few layers down and the domain will thrive in today's new era of engineering.

Now, it shouldn't have taken him $200 of Claude tokens to build a website, it looks good visually, but it's quite barebones... but the bones are there, and so is his golfing expertise! An average engineer would've taken maybe $20 worth of tokens to build what he had, and maybe in half the time. He had to go back and redo a bunch of things, along with not really understanding the limitations/usages of context. What's going to make engineers valuable are the ones who can _understand_, **really** understand the domain, the product, the *big picture*, **in addition to** the code itself. I try to harp this new methodology to my engineers, hoping that if anything, the concept of what AI can be as a software implementation tool sticks with them.
